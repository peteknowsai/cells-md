Hibernation: One Truth, One Decider

A comprehensive model for when cells sleep — and the two pieces of work left to get there.

Why this doc. Cells-not-sleeping was chased as a string of separate bugs — a welld state desync, a bridge that wouldn't reconnect after a thaw, an always-on cell that could hibernate itself. They are not separate. Every one is the same shape, and the fix is an architecture, not another patch.

The pattern behind every bug

Two failure shapes, over and over:

State tracked in more than one place
Whether a VM is running lives in lume (the truth), in welld's runtime.json, and in welld's in-memory cache. They drift, and an actuator trusts the stale copy. → the desync that pinned a cell awake; startWell never persisting alive_running; the in-memory staleness still snagging egg-0f7d66.
A decision owned by more than one party
"Should this cell sleep?" is decided both by the cell — which imperatively POSTs /sleep — and by welld — watchdog plus the never-sleep pin. The two disagree. → the pin getting bypassed; the cooperative-sleep change that would have hibernated the always-on cells.

Plus two structural gaps that let the above bite harder: the bridge had no clear owner for its own liveness, and birth didn't guarantee a cell could even hibernate.

The model — four invariants

The one move that matters. Invariant 2. Stop letting a cell command its own hibernation. busy and idle are the cell's entire vocabulary; welld decides and actuates. That single change collapses the whole second bug class — the pin bypass, the cell/welld tug-of-war, and the hazard class the reverted cooperative-sleep change exposed all disappear at once.

Invariant 2 — welld is the sole decider

Today the cell has two channels to welld: /lifecycle {busy|idle} (a hint) and /sleep (an imperative command). The command is the problem — it bypasses the pin, because welld's /sleep handler hibernates unconditionally while the pin (auto_sleep_seconds === null) is honored only by the watchdog. Collapse the two channels to one: the cell reports state, welld owns the decision.

Cells side — dna/cells/base/site/server.ts

  • Delete signalSleep(), scheduleSleepAfterGrace(), cancelPendingSleep(), pendingSleepTimer, SLEEP_GRACE_MS, and the POST /sleep call.
  • Keep signalLifecycle("busy"|"idle") — unchanged. onTranslatedLine: agent_end → signalLifecycle("idle") only; agent_start / prompt-receive → signalLifecycle("busy").
  • Net: the cell's hibernation vocabulary is two words. It never says "sleep."

Wells side — daemon/welld.ts (wells team)

  • Delete the /sleep route. The watchdog is already the decider — it consumes the busy/idle set, the lastTouched timer, auto_sleep_seconds, and the pin.
  • To keep fast cooperative sleep without the cell commanding it: on /lifecycle {idle}, record idle_since. The watchdog sleeps an idle-signalled cell after a short grace (≈8 s) instead of the full auto_sleep_seconds — still gated by the pin, seal-readiness, and the activity probe. The idle signal becomes a hint that lets welld sleep sooner, not a command that sleeps it regardless.
Effort
Cells side ~1 hr (deletion + test). Wells side is the wells team's call — delete the endpoint, optional idle-hint fast path.
Risk
Low. The watchdog is already proven in production (it hibernates idle cells correctly today). This removes a code path; it adds none.
Payoff
The pin becomes airtight. Tuning "how aggressively to pack cells per box" becomes a single knob — auto_sleep_seconds — instead of a negotiation between two deciders.

Invariant 4 — every cell born hibernate-ready

Shipped 2026-05-22. ensureHibernateReady() guards both birth paths — the pool claim and the special birth. Fleet audited: all eight cell wells are hibernate-ready (mother and pulse, the only two that weren't, are sealed). The scope below is the record of what was built.

bakePoolMember already seals each pool egg and fails loud if the seal fails — so a pool egg that finishes baking is sealed. The gap is downstream: a cell can still reach status: alive un-hibernate-ready — an egg baked before the seal-at-bake code, or one whose hibernate_ready was clobbered by a later substrate event — and nothing re-checks at birth. nfv-market-cc is the live proof: claimed, registered, alive, and unable to hibernate.

The guard — cli/cells.ts, the pool-claim path

  • After claiming a pool egg and before reg.cells.push (≈ line 2620), assert hibernate_ready === true on the claimed well via GET /v1/wells/<well>.
  • If false: call sealWell() then — a mid-life seal is ~8 s and proven to work (it sealed egg-0f7d66 cleanly). If the seal fails, reject the egg back to the pool as defective rather than registering a cell that can't hibernate.
  • No cell reaches alive that the hibernation system can't manage.

One-time cleanup

A fleet audit: scan every live cell, flag any with hibernate_ready = false, seal it. nfv-market-cc is one such cell today; there may be other pre-seal-code survivors.

Effort
~2 hr including the audit.
Risk
Low. sealWell mid-life is already proven in production.

Sequencing

  1. Invariant 4 first — it's self-contained, cells-only, and stops the bleed (no new unsealed cells, existing ones cleaned up).
  2. Invariant 2 next — cells-side deletion can land immediately (removing /sleep emission is safe on its own; the watchdog already covers the case). The wells-side /sleep removal + idle-hint fast path is coordinated with the wells team — they already have the /sleep-ignores-pin report.
  3. Invariant 1's last gap — the egg-0f7d66 in-memory staleness — is the wells team's, already handed over with evidence.

End state. One truth for whether a VM runs (lume). One decider for whether a cell sleeps (welld's watchdog), enforcing the pin in one place. One owner for the bridge (the supervisor, self-healing). Every cell hibernate-ready from birth. Hibernation stops being a source of bugs and becomes a single tunable knob.
