Cells.

A fleet of AI agents, each in its own little Linux machine.

Hand it a problem. A team spins up to do it — each agent in its own machine, the whole fleet on your Mac.

The idea

Hand it a problem.
It builds the team.

You don't wire up infrastructure or configure cloud services. You describe what needs doing — and a fleet of agents spins up to do it.

The unit

A cell is a little Linux machine.

One agent inside, its own files and memory and identity. Real hardware virtualization — not a container, not a sandbox-in-a-tab. The machine is the boundary.

The shape

A fleet, not a chatbot.

A Chief of Staff with cells under it, each owning a piece of the work. Drop into any cell, tune it, swap its model. Every cell is reachable and yours to shape.

Always on

Local-first. Always reachable.

Lives on your Mac — fast, no network tax between agents. Cloudflare marries the fleet to the network: your cells stay online even with the lid closed.

The layer underneath

Cells runs on wells.

Wells is the substrate — the daemon that owns the Linux VMs cells live in. It makes a little machine appear on demand; cells is the agent inside it.

They're two layers of one box. You bring up wells first, then cells births onto it. Cells cannot run without wells — it's a hard dependency, not an option.

Status — alpha

Being built in the open. The repo is real; the substrate isn't self-serve yet.

A working install needs wells running first, a proxy bridge that routes a cell's LLM calls through your own subscriptions, and two consumer subscriptions in good standing. None of that is one-command yet. The brief and the source are the entry points if you want to follow along.
