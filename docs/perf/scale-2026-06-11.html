Scale test — finding the Mini's ceiling

2026-06-11 · 48 GB / 18-core Mac · welld 5810274 · cells (refresh + steward + target=main branch)

Why

The pitch is "every buyer gets an advisor" — N cells, mostly asleep, woken by conversation. Nobody had birthed a batch and watched the machine sweat. This run measures the four pressure points: serial birth latency (mother is the bottleneck by design), pool drain/refill under birth pressure (the steward's refill loop vs a birth burst), concurrent wake storms (boot_gate=3, wake_gate=1), and all-awake memory footprint (1 GB/well committed while running).

Five warm-egg births in 165s wall — 32–36s each, dead consistent, mother fully serialized. The pool drains ~10× faster than it bakes (a birth claims in seconds; a bake takes ~90s), so any burst longer than the pool depth hits the drought path. With the same-day fix, the drought self-heals: the failed birth starts the refill, and the retry lands ~2–4 minutes later. Steward backstops at 30-minute intervals.

Verdict

This 48 GB Mini comfortably runs dozens of mostly-asleep cells with 10–15 simultaneous conversations. The three binding constraints, in order: RAM for concurrently-awake cells (~1.9 GB RSS each; hibernated cells cost zero — budget ~15–18 awake alongside macOS and services), disk per cell (5–9 GB; ~30–40 cells on current free space), and mother's serial birth throughput (~2 births/min while the pool holds, ~0.6/min at bake speed once drained — bursts longer than pool depth pay a ~2.5-minute drought-recovery gap, now self-healing). Wake-on-demand held under a 5-way storm at p50 5.7s, which is the number that matters for the every-buyer-gets-an-advisor pitch: a sleeping advisor answers WhatsApp in about six seconds.
